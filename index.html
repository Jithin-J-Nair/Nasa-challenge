Nothing on the Moon rusts. Nothing on the Moon blows away.

NASA’s Catalogue of Manmade Material on the Moon lists an Apollo 11 lunar-module descent stage. The source-audited dossier keeps only the catalogue statement and location that this site has checked.

Silent Fleet now distinguishes a NASA affiliation from NASA verification. Each dossier keeps narrative, technical figures, locations, status, and discovery claims hidden until every visible claim is matched to an opened NASA source. The full claim register is available in the project documentation.

NASA records are the default archive scope. Contextual records from other agencies are available only through the explicit Other agencies control, where they retain a source link but do not inherit verification from their agency label.

Beyond the planets, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 use a dated NASA status snapshot rather than extrapolated live trajectories. The Voyager section identifies the as-of date and displays rounded approximations.

My battery is low and it's getting dark. A poet's translation of Opportunity's final telemetry · 10 June 2018 · by Jacob Margolis

This archive exists because most people only ever hear about the missions that are still working. But the machines that stopped are the ones with the stories. They were built by people, launched on fire, and abandoned on purpose — and every one of them bought us something we did not have before.

Claim-level evidence

A mission name, agency label, or related page is not enough. A dossier is marked complete only when its displayed claims share an opened NASA source.

Failure is data

NASA/JPL’s Mars 3 record is retained because the opened source specifically reports its 14.5-second transmission. Other contextual records remain withheld until equally specific evidence is reviewed.

Images carry evidence too

An image is either artefact-specific, a clearly labelled mission image, or a placeholder. The site never uses a different spacecraft as a visual stand-in.

On board both Voyagers

A phonograph with no needle, thrown into an ocean with no shore.

Each Voyager carries a 30-centimetre gold-plated copper phonograph record, sealed in an aluminium jacket with a uranium-238 clock so a finder can date it. The cover is etched with instructions: how to build a needle, how fast to spin it, and — because the builders assumed whoever found it would not know where we are — a map.

Fourteen lines radiate from a point. They are the periods of fourteen pulsars, written in binary, in units of the hydrogen spin-flip transition. Any civilisation that knows pulsars can triangulate the Sun. Below it, the hyperfine transition of hydrogen gives the unit of length and time for everything else.

Audit rule 01
Agency affiliation is not claim verification.
Record-level reviewVisible copy must cite an opened NASA sourceMission narratives and technical figures remain hidden until every displayed claim in that record has been checked.
Audit rule 02
A related image is not an artefact image.
Image-level reviewArtefact-specific NASA image or labelled placeholderEach dossier identifies the image source or explicitly says that no artefact-specific image has been audited.
Audit rule 03
A catalogue row is not a total mass.
Moon inventory methodTop-level object/location rows onlyThe Moon inventory section preserves NASA’s own location text and states the counting method instead of estimating an unsupported mass.
Audit rule 04
Uncertainty stays visible.
Source pathEvery dossier has a mission-specific NASA referenceReaders can open the NASA page from the dossier and distinguish checked claims from withheld material.
