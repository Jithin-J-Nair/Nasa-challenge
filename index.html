    A source link is a beginning, not a permission slip.

    Silent Fleet separates a story moment from a factual record. The warm story voice invites a reader in; the NASA-checked section below it carries the facts, specifications, image treatment, and reading link.

    The project uses NASA’s Catalogue of Manmade Material on the Moon, mission pages, image records, and dated status pages. It keeps the Moon catalogue’s rows, quantities, and locations separate rather than converting them into a made-up grand total.

    NASA records appear first in the Field Guide. Other-agency context records stay available through an explicit filter, but a NASA-related page does not automatically make every detail on those records verified.

    Some records are intentionally short. Their source link remains open to readers while the archive withholds extra details until the evidence review is complete.

    Wonder is welcome here. Guesswork is not. Silent Fleet evidence rule

    On board both Voyagers

    A phonograph with no needle, thrown into an ocean with no shore.

    Each Voyager carries a 30-centimetre gold-plated copper phonograph record, sealed in an aluminium jacket with a uranium-238 clock so a finder can date it. The cover is etched with instructions: how to build a needle, how fast to spin it, and — because the builders assumed whoever found it would not know where we are — a map.

    Fourteen lines radiate from a point. They are the periods of fourteen pulsars, written in binary, in units of the hydrogen spin-flip transition. Any civilisation that knows pulsars can triangulate the Sun. Below it, the hyperfine transition of hydrogen gives the unit of length and time for everything else.

    What they taught us

    This section now renders only dossier summaries whose visible claims have been checked against an opened NASA source. The remaining historic copy is withheld rather than inferred from an agency label.

    Audit rule 01
    Agency affiliation is not claim verification.
    Record-level reviewVisible copy must cite an opened NASA sourceMission narratives and technical figures remain hidden until every displayed claim in that record has been checked.
    Audit rule 02
    A related image is not an artefact image.
    Image-level reviewArtefact-specific NASA image or labelled placeholderEach dossier identifies the image source or explicitly says that no artefact-specific image has been audited.
    Audit rule 03
    A catalogue row is not a total mass.
    Moon inventory methodTop-level object/location rows onlyThe Moon inventory section preserves NASA’s own location text and states the counting method instead of estimating an unsupported mass.
    Audit rule 04
    Uncertainty stays visible.
    Source pathEvery dossier has a mission-specific NASA referenceReaders can open the NASA page from the dossier and distinguish checked claims from withheld material.
